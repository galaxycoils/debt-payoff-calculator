The quiet trap

When the minimum does not cover interest

A $1,000 card at 24% APR costs $20 in interest this month. A $15 minimum means the balance is $5 higher after you “paid on time.” That is the trap.

The one-month test

  • Monthly interest ≈ balance × APR ÷ 12.
  • If the minimum is smaller than that number, principal goes up.
  • The extra that stops the bleed is exactly the gap: interest minus minimum.

You do not need a 30-year projection to see this. One month of arithmetic is enough to know whether the account is shrinking.

Why issuers like small minimums

A 1–3% of-balance minimum looks affordable. On a high APR it can sit under the interest line for years. The statement still says current. The balance does not.

What to do with the number

Add the gap to extra this month. That is not a payoff plan yet — it is the floor that keeps the hole from getting deeper. Then use snowball or avalanche to actually finish.
