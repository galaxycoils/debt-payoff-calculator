First twelve months

Year-one wins are why snowball feels faster

Avalanche usually wins the interest column over the full plan. Snowball often wins the screenshot people send a partner: two accounts closed before next summer. The calculator counts both.

What a “win” is

A win is a debt whose balance hits zero on or before month 12. Not a lower statement. Not utilization. An account you can close or park at zero.

Why the count diverges

  • Snowball pours extra into the smallest balance, so tiny cards die first.
  • Avalanche pours extra into the highest APR, which is often the large card that will not die this year.
  • On a barbell stack (one tiny bill, one monster APR) snowball can post two kills while avalanche posts one.

When avalanche still wins year one

If the highest-APR debt is also small, avalanche gets the early win and the cheaper interest. The card will say “tie” or “avalanche” and show the year-one interest gap so you are not guessing.

How to use the card

Run the calculator, then look at the year-one card under the 13th-payment split. Drag extra and watch the kill count jump. That jump is the retention loop: people stay to manufacture a second win before December.
