Paying 22% debt is a 22% return you cannot lose.

Investing extra while a credit card compounds is a bet that markets beat a guaranteed rate. Sometimes they do. Often they do not on the balances people actually carry.

What the calculator compares

It reruns your plan with the extra payment, then asks: if that same extra had been invested each month at your assumed return for the length of the payoff, how large would the pile be versus the interest you avoided?

  • Interest avoided = minimums-only interest minus plan interest.
  • Invest pile = future value of the extra as a monthly annuity.
  • Edge = interest avoided minus that pile. Positive means the debt wins.

A useful default

  • Cards and personal loans above ~10–12% APR: extra payments first.
  • 0% promo balances: investing extra can win if you still hit the promo end date.
  • Always keep a small cash cushion so a surprise does not become new debt.

Markets have down years. Your statement APR does not. Use the live return field on the results card and watch the winner flip.
