Cash-flow order

Should you kill the biggest bill first?

Snowball hunts small balances. Avalanche hunts high APR. Cash-flow first hunts the payment that hurts every payday. The calculator runs all three so you can pick a date, not a slogan.

What “highest minimum first” means

Every account still gets its minimum. Extra principal goes to the debt with the largest required payment until that account is gone. The next extra target is the next-largest remaining minimum.

Why people want it

  • A $180 car note vanishing is more visible than a $28 store card.
  • Freed minimums become extra that much earlier if that bill was large.
  • Budget conversations get easier when one line item disappears.

What it usually costs

If the big payment is a low-APR installment loan and a small card is at 24%, avalanche still saves more interest. Cash-flow first can finish later and cost more — unless the first freed minimum is so large that the snowball of extras catches up.

How to decide

Run the three-way card on your stack. If the calendars are within a month or two, pick the first-kill that you will actually celebrate. If avalanche is a year faster, pay the interest math, not the ego math.
