Overtime vs side hustle

Gross hourly pay lies. After-tax cash pointed at extras, divided by hours, tells you which extra shift is actually worth it.

Why “more hours” is the wrong scoreboard

A $30 overtime hour that lands as $22 after tax beats a $20 gig hour that lands as $16 — unless the gig takes twice as long to schedule, drive, and invoice. The calculator scores each path by interest saved per hour worked, not by who looks busier.

How to enter rates

  • Use take-home dollars, not the number on the job posting.
  • Count only hours you would actually work this month.
  • Leave commute and setup time in the hours if you would not work them otherwise.

Worked example

A $1,200, 0% balance with a $100 minimum takes 12 months. Four overtime hours at $25 after tax add $100 extra and cut the plan to 6 months. Eight hustle hours at $15 add $120 and also finish in 6 months — but they cost twice the hours. Overtime wins per hour even when the hustle sends more cash.

When the hustle still wins

High-APR cards punish delay. If the hustle can run more hours than your employer will approve for overtime, total interest saved can still favor the side work. Drag both sliders on the results card and watch the winner flip.
