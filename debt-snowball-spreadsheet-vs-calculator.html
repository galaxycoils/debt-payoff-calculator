Spreadsheet vs calculator

A debt snowball spreadsheet is a ledger. A calculator is a sandbox. Use both, but don’t build a 40-tab workbook just to answer “what if I paid $75 more?”

What a spreadsheet is good at

  • Logging actual payments you already made
  • Tracking remaining balances month to month
  • Sharing a file with a partner who lives in Excel

Where spreadsheets get expensive

Every extra payment, snowflake, raise, or strategy change means rewriting formulas or copying a new column. Interest accrual, minimums, and payoff order are easy to get subtly wrong. People abandon the sheet and go back to minimums.

What the calculator is for

Instant what-ifs: drag extra payment, add a tax-refund snowflake, compare snowball vs avalanche, price a raise. Numbers stay in the browser. Copy a scenario link when you want a second opinion.

A simple split

  • Plan the attack in the calculator.
  • Record what you actually paid in a one-tab sheet or your bank app.
  • Re-run the calculator when income or rates change — don’t rebuild the model.
