Daily burn

How much interest are you paying per day?

APR looks small. Divided by 365 it becomes a meter you can feel. The calculator turns every balance into dollars-per-sunrise and the age you will be when it stops.

The $1-a-day example

A $3,650 card at 10% APR accrues $365 of interest in a year if the balance never moves. That is exactly $1.00 every day. Two cards like that are $2.00. People who would not “waste a dollar” still watch this meter run because the statement hides it in a monthly line.

Why the clock is addictive

  • It updates when you drag extra payment — days-left drops in real time.
  • Debt-free age makes the date personal. “Age 34” hits harder than “March 2028.”
  • Hourly burn is tiny, which is the point: waiting is a drip, not a splash.

What the number is not

Daily interest on revolving credit is balance × APR ÷ 365 (issuers often use 365 or 360). It is not your minimum payment. Paying only the minimum can still let the daily meter stay almost the same for years.
