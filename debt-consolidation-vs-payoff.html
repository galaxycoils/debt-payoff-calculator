Debt consolidation vs just paying it off

A lower APR looks like a win until the origination fee and a longer term stretch the calendar. Run both plans on the same budget.

What consolidation actually changes

You replace several balances with one loan. The new rate is often lower than credit-card APRs. The new payment is set by the lender’s term. Fees get added to the balance you now owe.

  • Fee: 1–8% origination is common. On $10,000 that is $100–$800 you start behind.
  • Term: A 60-month loan can drop the payment and raise total interest if you only pay the new minimum.
  • Budget: The honest test is keeping the dollars you already send to creditors.

When the loan wins

High-APR revolving debt, a short-enough term that you still finish in a few years, and a fee small enough that interest savings cover it. Our calculator flags “worth it” only when net savings after fees are positive.

When staying put wins

If you already throw extra at avalanche, a modest rate cut plus a 3% fee can lose. If the loan payment is lower than your current total and you spend the difference, you often finish later and pay more.

Do not close the cards and reload them

Consolidation only works if the old accounts stop growing. Keep a written payoff plan. Model snowflakes and biweekly payments on either path before you sign.
