Are you on the plan you already built?

Most people run a calculator once and never look again. A two-field check-in — months elapsed and what you still owe — tells you if the curve is winning.

What “on track” means here

The calculator stores the month-by-month remaining balance in this browser after you hit Calculate. Later you type the months that passed and the sum of today’s balances. If you owe less than the curve, you are ahead. If you owe more, you are behind by that dollar gap.

Why this beats a spreadsheet

  • No new file. The plan already lives next to the debts you typed.
  • A $50 band keeps small rounding from screaming “behind.”
  • Paid-off early is called out as done, not as a chart error.

Utilization rides the same curve

If a debt looks like a card (or you store a limit), the same remaining-balance path estimates when revolving utilization crosses 30% and 10%. That date is the screenshot people send a partner.
