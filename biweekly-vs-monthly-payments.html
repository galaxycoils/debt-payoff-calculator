Biweekly vs monthly payments

There are 26 two-week periods in a year. Half a monthly payment, 26 times, is 13 full payments — one extra month of progress without “finding” a new budget line.

The simple math

If your combined minimums plus extra equal $P each month, twelve payments send $12P. Biweekly half-payments send $13P. Our engine adds P ÷ 12 as extra each month so a monthly amortization still matches that yearly total.

Worked example

A $1,300 balance at 0% APR with a $100 minimum takes 13 months. Flip biweekly and the same cash flow finishes in 12. With interest the gap is larger because the extra principal lands earlier.

When it backfires

If “biweekly” just means paying whenever a paycheck hits but the total cash per year does not rise, nothing changes. Autopay on the due date plus a planned extra is clearer than hoping timing saves you.
