0% intro APR pitfalls

A promo rate is a deadline, not a gift. Miss the window and the balance can jump to 20%+ overnight.

The fine print that actually costs money

  • Promo length is short. 12–18 months is common. Large balances do not finish on minimums.
  • Transfer fees are real interest. 3–5% upfront is prepaid interest even at 0% APR.
  • Deferred-interest store cards. If anything remains when the promo ends, some issuers charge interest back to day one.
  • Post-promo APR is often worse than the card you left.
  • One late payment can cancel 0%. The rate reverts and a penalty APR can stick.

Who a 0% offer helps

Someone with a written payoff date inside the promo window, a fee they already priced, and a budget that still covers every other minimum. If the calculator says you still have a balance in month 19, the offer is a trap unless you plan a second transfer (and another fee).

How to check before you apply

Enter the current debts. Use the target-date solver for the promo end month. If the extra payment required is more than you can keep for every month of the promo, skip the transfer or shrink the amount you move.

Then drag the APR-shock slider: if the promo ends and rates land 12 points higher, how many extra months appear? That number is the cost of finishing late.
