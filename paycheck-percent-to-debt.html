Paycheck math

How much of a paycheck should go to debt?

A dollar amount on a slider is abstract. A percent of take-home is something you can defend on payday. The leftover after minimums plus that extra is the real budget test.

The three numbers

  • Take-home for the month (after tax, not gross).
  • Minimums you already have to send.
  • The share you choose — 5%, 10%, 15% — turned into extra principal.

Leftover = take-home − minimums − extra. If leftover is negative, the percent is a fantasy. Drop it until rent and food still fit.

Why percent beats a round number

A $200 extra is easy to type and easy to abandon when a paycheck is thin. A 10% rule scales when hours drop or a raise lands. You can still override it with a snowflake in a flush month.

What “aggressive” actually means

20–30% of take-home on top of minimums is a sprint. It only works if leftover still covers housing and a small cash buffer. The calculator flags a tight leftover so you do not plan a collapse.
